What happens today

Family and group homes

  1. Prepare

    Complete the online applicant information session. Read the current requirements and select new licensing or an existing-home change.

  2. Send the initial package

    The published new-home process uses a mailed application, family assessment and direct-deposit form. Original criminal-record and vulnerable-sector checks for the applicant and adult residents go in separate sealed envelopes.

  3. Ministry screens local need

    The Ministry contacts applicants and assesses need. Screened-in applicants attend two initial information sessions, work on Learning Plans and provide references.

  4. Visits and preparation

    Work with the consultant on home visits, space and suitability. Arrange fire/heating inspections when advised. Prepare the handbook, menu, fee schedule, insurance, training evidence or plan and applicable assistant records.

  5. Final sessions and review

    Attend two final information sessions and complete any further visit or corrections. The Ministry decides and issues the licence. Elected grants have their own timing and approvals.

Centres

  1. Establish need and readiness

    Survey of Need is recommended. Develop partnerships, choose governance and complete the centre-development information session.

  2. Request development approval

    Email the current Community Needs Assessment and proposed development/operating budgets with applicable incorporation evidence. The Ministry acknowledges receipt and identifies missing information. Business plans and support letters can strengthen the request.

  3. Prepare licensing and applicable funding

    Following Ministry notification, work with the consultant on the centre licence application, applicable funding agreements and grants. Payment setup is separate from permission to operate.

  4. Premises, staffing and policies

    Arrange fire, health and heating reports. Prepare approved plans, insurance, applicable lease and governance evidence, parent/staff handbooks and menu. Fee schedules are submitted through the existing Enrolment Portal.

  5. Final consultant review

    The checklist calls for a final meeting at least four weeks before planned opening. The consultant measures final space and reviews compliance. The Ministry’s operating licence and funding decisions remain distinct.

Three decisions to keep separate: readiness or development approval, an operating licence, and funding. A completed checklist or a locally prepared application is not an issued licence.

The proposed improvement

The following is a service-design proposal. It does not claim that a new local form replaces an official submission or that government systems already support these changes.

Current evidenceProposed experienceResponsibility retained
Organization/contact details and requested age-band spaces recur in centre assessment, licence and budget forms.Enter shared information once; prefill appropriate forms. Keep legal entity, site and payment-supplier names distinct.Applicant confirms accuracy; Ministry approves licensed spaces.
Different forms, supporting documents and submission routes serve different purposes.A single facility record with four clear stages: provider details, application package, preparation to open and final review. Show the right forms for the selected route.Official rules and submission routes remain visible.
Applications may require further information and corrections.Ask for a correction against the exact answer or document. Preserve prior versions and show who is expected to act next.Applicant answers; assigned reviewer accepts or requests changes.
Funding requests contain conditions and may occur before or after licensing.Offer grants separately; let applicants choose or decline. Reuse relevant identity and planning details without merging approvals.Human funding decision; licensing does not depend on an optional claim.
Checks, references, inspections and bank confirmations are completed by other parties.Track requested, sent, received, correction needed and accepted. Show outside dependencies alongside applicant tasks.Police, references, bank, inspector and Ministry retain their own judgments and attestations.
Public templates are available for policy and narrative drafting.Offer clearly labelled drafting help for low-risk descriptions, with source links and applicant review before use.Do not generate suitability judgments, inspections, references, signatures or approval decisions.
Some current templates are consultant-provided or unavailable publicly.Mark these as “current template needed” and track receipt. Add exact questions only after the authentic form is obtained.Ministry supplies or confirms the form.

Expected improvements are design intentions, not measured time savings. Research verifies overlap in fields and requested documents; it does not establish that staff always require the same file to be resubmitted.

Current version cautions
  • New group homes start at up to 12. Existing eligible group homes may apply to expand to 16; the two-year regular-licence and other approval conditions matter.
  • Home self-assessment still prints 40 hours of ECE training. Current regulation 61 requires a 45-hour introductory course or approved equivalent within one year; group providers need ECE I within three years, subject to applicable exceptions.
  • Parent handbooks are required for homes and centres. Both current information sheets say to use the supplied template, then customize the policies. This differs from optional budget and fee-schedule templates.
  • Current centre CNA is four pages. An older indexed three-page copy omits newer readiness questions. Use the live official link.
  • Centre licence form is still EY10/2021. Current regulation determines conditional incorporation, lease and ownership evidence. The form lacks a municipality-specific signature label.
  • Funding versions vary. Current tuition form is EY02/2026; PD application is 2026–27, while its general information sheet remains 2025–26. Confirm corresponding-year conditions before claiming.
  • Current direct-deposit form is Rev12/23. Certified or auditable electronic signatures are accepted by its instructions; this does not waive original police-check requirements.

Timing, boundaries and unresolved details

Published expectations

The home FAQ says applicants should hear from staff within 10 business days and describes an average licensing time of three to five months. Local-need screening can affect whether an application proceeds; a name may be kept on file for up to three months in a well-served area.

Centre applications remain open year-round, with development approvals focused on greatest need. The final-meeting instruction is at least four weeks before anticipated opening. No fixed total centre processing guarantee was verified.

Before using this in production

Confirm current consultant forms, local approval requirements, accepted electronic submissions and signatures, and the exact integration with existing fee/enrolment systems. Validate current grant conditions and any source-version conflicts.

The proposed four stages organize the experience; they are not claimed to be official Ministry stage names. Form mapping does not decide suitability, grant eligibility or regulatory compliance.

Banking, household/health details, police checks, references and signatures need controlled handling. Drafting assistance should avoid these records and should never score applicant suitability.

Grants are conditional. Home fire/health/safety funding is generally paid after fire inspection and before opening; start-up funding follows licence issuance. Active Play requires a licensed home; Play and Exploration requires the home provider’s Phase I evidence. Centre development grants need separate approvals. Ask for approval before purchases where the programme requires it.
