PART II - LICENSING 
DURATION OF LICENCES 
 
SECTION 7 
 
Unless a licence is suspended or cancelled pursuant to section 18 of the Act, the licence 
is valid: 
 
(a)  for the period specified in the licence; or 
 
(b)  if no period is specified in the licence, for a period of one year after the day 
on which the licence was issued. 
 
 22 May 2015 cC-7.31 Reg 1 s7. 
 
INTENT 
 
The intent of this section is to ensure: 
 all licences have an expiry date; 
 all facilities have an annual review of licensing standards; and 
 licensed child care services are reliable and available to parents who require the 
service. 
 
 
POLICY 
 
Child care facility licences shall be renewed annually as part of an annual 
assessment of licensing standards for facilities. 
 
Child care facilities shall be licensed to provide the equivalent of full-time, full 
year child care services unless otherwise approved by the Director and specified 
on the licence.  
 
Should a licensee wish to provide services for less than full-time or less than a 
full year, the licensee shall make application for a licence for specific hours 
and/or weeks/months of service.  
 
Refer to “What if you want to place your facility on hold?” in General Information 
for additional information.

For the purposes of this section: 
 “full-time” means a minimum of eight hours per day and five days per week 
(excluding Provincial statutory holidays and two PD days); and 
 “full year” means 52 weeks in the case of a child care centre and 48 weeks 
in the case of a child care home. 
 
Professional Development Day Closures 
Facilities may close for up to two (2) full days or up to four half days per licensing year 
for professional development (PD). A half day means a morning or afternoon. Other 
combinations, including partial days, are not permissible.  
 
Days are not cumulative and cannot be carried over into the next licensing year.  
 
Professional development days are not transferrable to alternate days if professional 
development opportunities are delivered when the facilty is normally closed.  
 
Facilities are not required to reimburse parent fees or to find alternate care during these 
days. Facilities  must provide parents and guardians with a minimum of six (6) weeks’ 
notice prior to closing for PD days.  
 
Refer to Sections 41, 42, 61 and 63 for acceptable learning topics for PD days. 
 
 
GUIDELINE 
 
Facilities are to ensure that families have access to continuous, reliable care 
services by providing full-time, full year care. In instances where boards or 
licensees wish to reduce the availability of child care services to accommodate 
lower attendance, facilties are to ensure the following: 
 child care within the facility is available for those parents who require care, 
and staffed accordingly; or  
 parents are able to find alternate care arrangements for their child, parents 
are reimbursed accordingly. 
 
These options also ensure that parents are not providing duplicate payments for 
child care services (i.e., paying parent fees for the facility in addition to fees for 
alternate care arrangements). The parent is responsible for any difference 
between the fee charged for alternate care and the amount reimbursed by the 
facility for alternate care.

PART II - LICENSING 
APPLICATION FOR LICENCE, RENEWAL – CENTRES  
 
SECTION 8 
 
(1)  An applicant for a licence to operate a centre must submit an application to the 
ministry on a form supplied by the ministry, together with: 
 
(a) a report from the public health officer respecting the sanitation, lighting, 
ventilation and general health and safety standards of the premises in 
which the centre will be operated; 
 
(b) a report from the Fire Commissioner’s local assistant respecting the fire 
safety standards of the premises in which the centre will be operated; 
 
(c) a report from a person acceptable to the Director respecting the heating 
system in the premises in which the centre will be operated; 
 
(d) if the applicant is a corporation or a co-operative, its certificate of 
incorporation or registration; 
 
(e) if the applicant is a corporation that is incorporated, registered or 
continued pursuant to The Business Corporations Act, 2021, a list of the 
names and addresses of the shareholders of the corporation; 
 
(f) if the applicant is a partnership, a list of the names and addresses of the 
partners; 
 
(g) if the applicant is an individual, the applicant’s health services number, if 
requested by the Director; and, 
 
(h) any other information that the Director may request. 
  
(2)  An applicant for the renewal of a licence to operate a centre must submit to the 
ministry: 
 
(a) the reports described in clauses (1)(a) to (c); and, 
 
(b) any other information that the Director may request.

PART II - LICENSING 
CHILD CARE SPACES - CENTRES 
 
SECTION 11 
 
(1) A licence for a centre must specify the maximum number of licensed child care 
spaces that the licensee is authorized to provide in the centre.  
 
(2) The maximum number of licensed child care spaces that may be authorized for 
one centre is 90. 
 
(3) A licence for a centre must specify the maximum number of licensed child 
care spaces that are allocated as: 
 
(a) infant child care spaces; 
 
(b) toddler child care spaces; 
 
(c) preschool child care spaces; 
 
(d) school-age child care spaces; and 
 
(e) flexible child care spaces. 
 
(4) If a centre provides child care services to infants, the maximum number of 
licensed child care spaces that may be allocated as infant child care spaces is 18. 
 
22 May 2015 cC-7.31 Reg 1 s11; 1 Aug 2025 
SR 60/2025 s11; 21 Aug 2026 SR 55/2026 s6. 
INTENT 
 
 The intent of this section is to ensure: 
• all centre licences specify the number of flexible child care spaces, the number of 
spaces allocated to each age category, and the number of subsidized spaces; and 
• appropriate environments for children by limiting the maximum number of child 
care spaces in a centre. 
 
 
POLICY 
 
The intent of child care spaces allocated as flexible across age groups (excluding infants) 
is to enable centres to better meet the changing child care needs of the community.

Enrollment of a child prior to Age Eligibility  
 
Child care facilities may request approval to enrol children prior to age eligibility under special 
or exceptional circumstances to provide short-term flexibility.  
 
These requests will be considered on a case-by-case basis and must demonstrate that all 
regulatory requirements, including space, staffing, and care provisions, can be fully met. 
Approval of these requests is temporary. Examples of early entrance include, but are not 
limited to: 
• Enrollment of a child under two weeks of age in Teen Support Centre 
• Enrollment of a child under six weeks of age in an infant space 
• Enrollment of a child under 18 months of age in a centre not licensed for infant care 
• Approvals will generally be limited to one (1) month, and no more than two (2) months 
in exceptional circumstances.

PART II - LICENSING 
AGE GROUPINGS - CENTRES 
 
SECTION 12 
 
A licence for a centre must specify whether the centre is authorized to provide 
child care services only to children in groups of single age categories or whether 
the centre is authorized to provide child care services to children in groups of 
mixed age categories. 
 
 22 May 2015 cC-7.31 Reg 1 s12. 
 
 
INTENT 
 
The intent of this section is to allow child care centres to provide appropriate 
programming.  
 
 
GUIDELINES 
 
A general assessment of the facility to authorize mixed age categories ensures the 
following: 
 Staff-to-child ratios and group size are adhered to. 
 Appropriate equipment and furnishings are available to accommodate the needs 
of the children in attendance. 
 Policies and procedures are established to promote a safe, healthy and quality 
environment. 
 The physical environment is varied enough to suit the developmental diversity of 
the children in attendance. 
 Daily program planning is appropriate for all age groups. 
 Supervision is adequate to ensure the safety of all children in attendance.
