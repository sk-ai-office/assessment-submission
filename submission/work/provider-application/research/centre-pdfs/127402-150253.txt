What is a Parent Handbook and why do I need one? 
It is a requirement for child care facilities to develop operational policies and procedures 
and include these in a written Parent Handbook. A copy of the Parent Handbook should be 
provided to each parent. The Parent Handbook ensures that facilities have policies and 
procedures that are clear and consistent and that families are aware of all expectations. 
 
What is the Parent Handbook template? 
The Parent Handbook template was developed to assist you in writing a Parent Handbook. 
This template includes all information that must be included in the Parent Handbook 
according to The Child Care Regulations, 2015.  
 
Do I need to use the template to create my Parent Handbook? 
Yes, you need to use the Parent Handbook template. This template serves as a basic guide 
for what to include in a Parent Handbook. There are other sections you may choose to add 
that are relevant to the unique operation of the child care centre. You are encouraged to 
include additional sections as needed. 
  
How do I use the Parent Handbook template? 
Start by saving the Parent Handbook Template directly onto your computer. You are 
encouraged to revise your handbook to reflect changes in your policies, as needed. For 
each required component, the appropriate section of the Child Care Licensee’s Manual is 
referenced. By referring to the Child Care Licensee’s Manual, you will find further detail for 
each required component.  
 
Use this information sheet to complete the Child Care Centre Parent Handbook Template. 
Below there are questions for your consideration for each section of the Parent Handbook 
template. As you reflect on these questions, your answers may support you to write your 
Parent Handbook. Once your Parent Handbook is complete, ensure you provide a copy to 
the families of the children attending the child care centre.

Purpose of the Parent Handbook 
 
The Parent Handbook sets out the policies and procedures for the operation of a child 
care centre. The Parent Handbook assists parents to understand the child care service 
and serves to guide expectations of the parents of children who attend the centre.  
The policies and procedures in the Parent Handbook establish how the child care 
service may be used. Several policies and procedures are developed as requirements of 
child care legislation. Other policies and procedures may be developed that are unique 
to the operation of this child care centre.

Philosophy Statement 
Requirement: A written philosophy statement must be developed for the centre (refer to 
the Child Care Licensee’s Manual – Part III – Section 14 for further information). 
This is a statement about your early learning and child care values. Consider: 
• What do you believe is most important about educating and nurturing children? 
• What do you think is important for children to learn? (e.g. problem solving, social 
skills, personal responsibility, independence, creativity, etc.) 
• What is the centre’s code of ethics?

Daily Schedule 
Requirement: The centre must plan and implement a daily program that is consistent 
with the philosophy statement and is developmentally appropriate for children attending 
the centre (refer to the Child Care Licensee’s Manual – Part III – Section 14 for further 
information).   
Describe your daily schedule. This will inform parents about what their child will 
routinely experience (e.g. mealtimes, rest times, etc.). Consider: 
• How are the families expected to drop off and pick up children? Is there a sign in 
sheet? Are there requirements in situations where a custody agreement is in 
place? 
• How is the day structured? Do you provide for the following: 
o Active/quiet play? 
o Individual/group activity? 
o Indoor/outdoor activity? 
o Structured/free choice activity? 
• When are meals and snacks scheduled?  
• When are rests scheduled? Are they appropriate for nappers and non-nappers? 
• Do you have a predictable routine? 
• Do you have activities that are appropriate for different ages? 
 
 
Child Management 
Requirement: A licensee must implement child management practices that allow  
children to learn independence and socially appropriate behaviour and teaches self-
discipline rather than disciplining the children. The following practices are not permitted 
forms of child management: corporal punishment, physical, emotional or verbal abuse, 
denial of necessities, isolation, inappropriate physical or mechanical restraint (refer to 
the Child Care Licensee’s Manual – Part III – Section 15 for further information). 
The goal of child management is to support children’s development of positive self-
regulation and appropriate behaviour. Describe the forms of child management that are 
not permissible in the centre, and how you positively manage children’s behaviour. 
Consider: 
• How do you help children develop self-control?   
• What age-appropriate expectations do you have for each age of child?  
• What techniques do you use to guide children and support appropriate 
behaviour? 
• How have you organized the centre to promote positive child interactions and 
minimize inappropriate behaviour (e.g. adequate play space, space to be alone, 
etc.)? 
• Include a statement regarding your program’s commitment to the Saskatchewan 
Child Abuse Protocol.

Program Operation 
Requirement: A licensee must develop written policies and procedures for the 
operation for the child care centre (refer to the Child Care Licensee’s Manual – Part II – 
Section 7 and Part III – Section 16 for further information). 
Describe your program. Consider: 
• What days and hours do you operate?  
• What statutory holidays does the centre observe? 
• Describe closures for professional development days. Facilities must provide at 
least six (6) weeks’ notice prior to closing for professional development days.  
• What is the procedure for non-school days or early dismissal? 
• What services do you offer? 
o What are the ages of children you will provide care for? 
o Do you offer specialized services? 
o What is your program’s approach to personal health care? 
o Do you transport children? 
o Do you take children to extracurricular activities? 
o Are you located near any schools? Do you walk children to school? Are 
children bused? If so, who arranges busing? 
• Describe how you will engage families in your child care program. Consider: 
o Do you provide opportunities for parents to share their skills, culture, etc.?  
o Do you have an annual family day or special family events? 
o Does the centre require fundraising assistance from families?  
o Are parents required to volunteer time?  
o How will parents be involved in outings and activities? 
o How do you communicate effectively with families? 
o How will your program and environment support your philosophy described 
above? 
o How will you support/work in partnership with the parents/guardians in your 
program?  
• Describe guidelines for inclement weather (temperature, air quality, etc.) 
• Describe procedures for fire drills, emergencies evacuations, lock downs etc.  
• Describe the process for reporting complaints/concerns.

Requirement: A licensee must enter into a written agreement with a parent for the 
provision of child care services. Agreements are updated as changes occur (e.g. change 
in fees or hours of care). Refer to the Child Care Licensee’s Manual – Part III – Section 17

Describe how changes are made to the agreement that you enter into with parents for

• What notice is expected if there is a change in care arrangements? (e.g. full-time 
to daily care).   
• What notice is given to parents for fee increases when a child turns six, changes

schedule changes).

Termination of Child Care Services 
Requirement: A licensee must enter into a written agreement with a parent for the 
provision of child care services (refer to the Child Care Licensee’s Manual – Part III –

Describe how child care services are terminated. Consider: 
• What communication and how much written notice can the parent expect from

• Under what circumstance might you terminate a contract?  
• How much written notice do you require from parents when they want to

Fee Schedule and Payment 
Requirement: A licensee must have a written schedule of fees and give advance notice 
to parents of any changes to fees. Parents are charged the same fees for the same 
service (e.g. the infant child care fee is the same for all parents with infants). Refer to the 
Child Care Licensee’s Manual – Part III – Section 18 for further information. 
Provide your fee schedule. For all children under six years of age, fees are set to $217.50 
for full time care and $10 per day for children attending fewer than 10 days per month. 
Describe when and how payment is received. Consider: 
• Do the school-age fees fluctuate during summer months?  
• Are there fees charged for late payments or late pick-ups? 
• Do you accept cash, cheque or e-Transfer?  
• Are there applicable membership fees?  
• When do you provide receipts?

Health Policies 
Requirement: If a licensee suspects a child has a Category I or II communicable 
disease, the licensee must inform a designated public health officer and follow any 
recommendations to ensure the health and well-being of children attending the centre 
(refer to the Child Care Licensee’s Manual – Part III – Section 26 for further information). 
Describe policies and procedures for communicable diseases. Consider: 
• How is the transmission of communicable diseases reduced? 
• When will children not be permitted to attend as a result of illness? 
• How will you monitor children’s health and well-being? 
• What are the reporting practices when a child is injured at the child care centre? 
 
Administration of Medication  
Requirement:  
(1) Written Authorization: If a licensee agrees to administer medication, written 
authorization on a form supplied by the ministry must be obtained. On the same form, a 
written record of each dose of medication must be made. All non-emergency 
medications must be stored in a locked enclosure (refer to the Child Care Licensee’s 
Manual – Part III – Section 27(1) for further information). 
(2) Oral Authorization: In exceptional circumstances, a licensee may administer non-
prescription medication on the oral authorization of the parent of the child. Written 
authorization must be obtained as soon as possible in these circumstances (refer to the 
Child Care Licensee’s Manual – Part III – Section 27(2) for further information). 
Describe policies and procedures for administration of medication. Consider: 
• Do you administer medication? 
• What are the guidelines for the administration of medication? 
• How and where is medication locked?

Injuries and Unusual Occurrences  
Requirement: If a child in a regulated child care facility sustains an injury or is involved 
in an unusual or unexpected occurrence, it is a requirement to notify parents/guardians, 
ministry staff and submit a written report to the parent/guardian, Ministry of Education 
and the board of directors (refer to the Child Care Licensee’s Manual – Part III – Division 
3 – Section 34 for further information).  
Explain the process and requirements for documenting and reporting injuries and 
unusual occurrences.  
• What are some examples of the injuries and unusual occurrences for 
parents/guardians? 
• How does the reporting process differ for a minor injury compared to an unusual 
occurrence? 
• How are these situations investigated? 
• What measures does the centre take to avoid reoccurrence of these events? 
 
Confidentiality 
Requirement: All facilities must maintain the confidentiality of personal information of 
children attending the centre, including files, documents or any other records. 
Confidential information must not be disclosed without a parent’s written permission, 
except as required for the health and safety of children, or as required by law (refer to the 
Child Care Licensee’s Manual – Part III – Section 40 for further information).  
Describe how you will ensure confidentiality is maintained to protect children and 
families’ personal information. Consider: 
• How will you maintain the confidentiality of records? 
• How will you maintain the confidentiality of information verbally shared with you? 
• Please include the following statement to ensure all families are aware of the 
information sharing that occurs between regulated child care facilities and the 
Ministry of Education with respect to funding.  
• “As a condition of the Parent Fee Grant to reduce child care fees for 
Saskatchewan families, the Ministry of Education is required to gather 
information including child’s name, current fee and parent information to 
know who will receive what amount of funding. This information will be 
collected and used in accordance with The Freedom of Information and 
Protection of Privacy Act (FOIP).”
