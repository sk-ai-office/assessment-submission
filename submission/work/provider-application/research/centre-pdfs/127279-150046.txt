Setting Full-Time Monthly and Daily Fee Schedules 
New Child Care Centres - April 1, 2026 
 
 
Saskatchewan is committed to making life more affordable for families and supporting child care 
facilities to deliver quality early learning and child care services.  
 
Since April 1, 2023, funding from the Canada-Saskatchewan Canada-Wide Early Learning and Child 
Care Agreement (Agreement) has been invested to reduce parent fees for regulated child care in 
Saskatchewan to $10 a day or $217.50 per month for children who are under six year old.  
 
The Governments of Canada and Saskatchewan have signed a five-year extension to this agreement 
to March 31, 2031. The extension agreement, which came into effect April 1, 2026, allows 
Saskatchewan to provide $10 a day child care for Kindergarten children who turn six on or after 
April 1, 2026 until the end of that school year in June or when they turn seven, whichever comes 
first.  
Child care facilities set their own full-time monthly and daily fee schedules within an allowable 
range. Parents with children under six years of age or are attending Kindergarten pay $217.50 per 
month or $10 per day towards this fee, while the remainder of the fee is provided by the Ministry of 
Education through a Parent Fee Reduction Grant. Please see the Child Care Licensee’s Manual, 
Section 83.2.  
 
There is a cap on the Parent Fee Reduction Grant, which means there is also an upper limit to the 
fee schedules new facilities can set for infant, toddler and preschool spaces. This upper limit 
ensures that child care facilities in the province are provided equitable financial support. 
 
There are no upper fee limits for school-age space fee schedules since these spaces are not eligible 
for the Parent Fee Reduction Grant.  
 
The full-time monthly fee schedule you set is made up of the parent fee of $217.50, which 
parents pay out of pocket, plus the Parent Fee Reduction Grant, which the Ministry of 
Education provides directly to your child care centre. Parents are charged a full-time monthly 
fee when their child is enrolled 10 or more days per month. 
The daily fee schedule you set is made up of the parent fee of $10, which parents pay out of 
pocket, plus the Parent Fee Reduction Grant, which the Ministry of Education provides 
directly to your child care centre. Parents are charged a daily fee when their child is enrolled 
fewer than 10 days per month.

Child Care Facilities will receive a Parent Fee Reduction Grant for each child enrolled up to the total 
number of spaces permitted on a facility’s licence. The maximum monthly Parent Fee Reduction 
Grant encourages facilities to ensure all child care spaces are utilized.

What is a fee schedule limit? 
A fee schedule is a child care facility’s per space rate before the Parent Fee Reduction Grant is 
applied, and includes two components: 
 The parent fee of $217.50 monthly (or $10 per day) that the parent pays directly to the child 
care facility. 
 The Parent Fee Reduction Grant, which the Ministry of Education provides directly to the 
child care facility. 
A fee schedule limit is the maximum per space rate a child care facility is permitted to set for 
children under the age of six or until they complete Kindergarten and are enrolled in an infant, 
toddler, or preschool space. New facilities can set fee schedules at or below, but not above, the fee 
schedule limits.  
 
Why can’t I set any fee I choose? 
Fee schedule limits for new facilities are required in order to make child care funding equitable and 
sustainable in our province. Equitable means that the fees a new facility sets are comparable to the 
fees in current operational facilities. Fee limits ensure that funding for child care is fairly distributed 
across the province.  
 
How were the fee schedule limits determined? 
The Ministry of Education determined fee schedule limits by looking at all of the fee schedules in 
the province for centres in community groupings. The fee limits were calculated from actual fee 
schedules from all of the province’s operational child care centres. 
The fee schedule limits are set at the 80th percentile of current operational spaces. The 80th 
percentile means that 80 per cent of current facilities have fee schedules at or below that level. See 
the illustration below. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
How much can I expect to receive in Parent Fee Reduction Grants? 
The amount of Parent Fee Reduction Grant funding you are eligible to receive is equal to the fee 
schedule you set minus the parent fee up to the number of licensed child care spaces. The portion 
the parent pays you directly is always $217.50 per month for full-time child care, or $10 per day for 
daily child care.

For example, a child care centre operator in the Northern Saskatchewan Administrative District may 
choose to set a monthly infant fee of $945.00. This child care centre operator can expect to receive 
$727.50 from the Ministry of Education for an infant enrolled full-time ($945.00 - $217.50 = 
$727.50). 
 
Why are fee schedule limits different for Regina, Saskatoon, their immediate surrounding 
communities and the Northern Saskatchewan Administration District compared to the rest of the 
province? 
The limits reflect regional differences between current fee schedules in Saskatoon, Regina, 
immediate surrounding areas and the North compared to all other areas of the province.  
 
What if a family needs more or less than ten hours per day of care?  
If parents need more than ten hours of care a day (, facilities can provide this care as part of their 
Child Care Services Agreement with that parent. Facilities can choose to charge parents for hours of 
care over ten hours, but they do not have to. The choice is theirs based on their own operational 
needs. There are caps on the amount facilities can charge parents for extra care. Any extra fee 
charges need to be incorporated into the Child Care Services Agreement for parents requiring more 
than ten hours of care daily. 
To learn more about standard hours of care and extended hours fees, see additional information 
listed below.  
 
What are some considerations I should think about when setting my fee schedule? 
Facilities are encouraged to establish a budget and forecast their ongoing operational expenses to 
determine their fee schedules. Consideration should also be given to the other grant supports 
provided by the ministry, including (but not limited to) Early Childhood Services and Early Childhood 
Educator Wage Enhancement grants.
