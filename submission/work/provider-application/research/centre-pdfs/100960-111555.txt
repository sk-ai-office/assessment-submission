PART IV – STANDARDS FOR CENTRES  
DIVISION 1 - STAFF 
CHILD CARE WORKERS 
 
SECTION 42 
 
(1)  A licensee of a centre must ensure that all child care workers employed in 
the centre are at least 16 years of age. 
 
(2) A licensee of a centre must ensure that: 
 
(a) any person who commenced employment in the centre as a child 
care worker before January 1, 2002 and who continues to be 
employed in the centre as a child care worker for 65 hours or more 
per month completed, within six months of commencing 
employment, an orientation course for child care workers offered 
through Saskatchewan Polytechnic or an equivalent orientation or 
training course; 
  
(b) all persons who commence employment in the centre on or after 
January 1, 2002 as child care workers for 65 hours or more per month 
meet or exceed the qualifications of an early childhood educator I; 
 
(c) 30 per cent of the persons employed in the centre as child care 
workers for 65 hours or more per month meet or exceed the 
qualifications of an early childhood educator II; 
 
(d) in addition to the persons mentioned in clause (c), a further 20% of 
the persons employed in the centre as child care workers for 65 hours 
or more per month meet or exceed the qualifications of an early 
childhood educator III; and 
 
(e) each child care worker employed in the centre participates in at least 6 
hours per year of continuing education that is acceptable to a program 
consultant. 
 
(3) In calculating the percentages mentioned in clauses (2)(c) and (d), the 
centre director and any supervisors are to be included.

INTENT 
 
The intent of this section is to ensure child care workers have the required skills, 
knowledge and competencies to provide appropriate environments for children. 
 
POLICY 
 
All individuals employed 65 hours or more per month to provide child care services in a 
centre, shall make application for certification.   
 
Only post-secondary level courses will be considered for certification. 
 
Only course work completed through an accredited public college or university will be 
considered for certification. 
 
Transcripts from post-secondary institutions within Canada or the United States must be 
sent directly by the institution to the Ministry of Education, Early Childhood Educator 
Certification.  
 
For post-secondary institutions outside of North America, a course-by-course evaluation 
must be completed by World Education Services (WES). WES must send this evaluation 
directly to Ministry of Education, Early Childhood Educator Certification. 
 
It shall be the responsibility of the licensee of a centre to provide verification of child 
care workers’ participation in  six hours of continuing education at the annual review. 
 
Summer Relief  
 
By way of ministerial exemption, regulated child care centres are exempt from the 
minimum training requirements of subsection 42(2)(b) and subsection 44(2) of The Child 
Care Regulations, 2015 for temporary child care staff hired to provide vacation relief of 
regular child care staff during May, June, July or August. 
 
The following parameters will be applied when child care centres are hiring 
underqualified relief staff for summer months: 
• All staff counted in ratios must be 16 years of age or older. 
• There should be at least as many trained staff (ECE l, ll and lll) as there are 
untrained staff.  
• There should be at least as many permanent staff as there are temporary staff 
with exemptions. 
• An orientation must be provided to new staff.

• Summer relief staff who have not completed first aid and CPR courses in

In addition, centres may respond to the need for full-time school-age care for the

GUIDELINES 
 
Classification as an ECE I is granted upon successful completion of a 9-credit unit 
Orientation to early childhood course through Saskatchewan Polytechnic or 9 credit 
units of equivalent education or training. 
 
Classification as an ECE II is granted upon successful completion of a one-year early 
childhood certificate from a recognized college in Canada or 39 credit units of 
equivalent education or training. 
 
Classification as an ECE III is granted upon successful completion of a two-year early 
childhood diploma from a recognized college in Canada or 81 credit units of equivalent 
education or training. 
 
Further information about Early Childhood Educator Certification, including an 
application for certification, may be found online on the Government of Saskatchewan 
website.   
 
Calculating Staff ECE Percentages in a Centre 
Anyone who is working 65 hours or more in a month is counted as one staff person. 
 
Example:  
Centre with 10 Staff Working 65 Hours or More per Month (who are all at least 16 years 
of age and have at least an ECE I): 
• Two staff need to have at least an ECE III (Director and any Supervisor included); 
• Three staff need to have at least an ECE II (Director and any Supervisor not 
included); 
• Five remaining staff need to have an ECE I. 
 
Note:  A centre Director responsible for two facilities shall be counted in the facility in 
which they spend the majority of their time.

Continuing Education 
Continuing education opportunities should be from reputable sources like post-
secondary institutions and Ministry of Education sponsored events. Acceptable topics for 
continuing education are those that build core skills to support the quality of the 
facility’s early learning program including: 
 
• Learning that supports compliance with licensing requirements. 
• Implementation of Play and Exploration: Early Learning Program Guide and Essential 
Learning Experiences. 
• Building language and literacy skills with children, including techniques that 
encourage language development for infants and toddlers, use of reasoning among 
preschool children, and active listening and warm communication styles of 
educators. 
• Building relationships with families, including regular progress updates to families 
about their children. 
• Planning and or documenting using developmentally appropriate curriculum. 
• Assessing children using tools like the Ages and Stages Questionnaire to track a 
child’s developmental progress. 
• Responding to the needs of children with additional needs. 
• Building cultural competence, with a focus on Indigenous culture.  
 
Educator self-care or staff appreciation are not acceptable topics for continuing 
education.
