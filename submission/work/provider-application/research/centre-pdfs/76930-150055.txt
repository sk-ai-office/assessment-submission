PART IV – STANDARDS FOR CENTRES  
DIVISION 1 - STAFF 
CENTRE DIRECTOR AND SUPERVISOR  
 
SECTION 41 
 
(1) A licensee of a centre must appoint: 
 
(a) a centre director to be responsible for the day-to-day operations of the 
centre; and 
 
(b) a supervisor to act in place of the centre director in the centre director’s 
absence. 
 
(2) A licensee of a centre must ensure that an individual who is appointed as a 
centre director: 
 
(a) is at least 18 years of age; and 
 
(b) subject to subsection (4), meets or exceeds the qualifications 
of an early childhood educator III. 
 
(3) A licensee of a centre must ensure that an individual who is appointed as a 
supervisor: 
 
(a) is at least 18 years of age; and 
 
(b) meets or exceeds the qualifications of an early childhood educator I. 
 
(4) An individual who was appointed as a centre director before July 1, 2001 is 
not required to meet the qualifications of an early childhood educator III, 
but he or she must meet, as a minimum, the qualifications of an early 
childhood educator II. 
 
(5) A licensee of a centre must ensure that each individual who is appointed as 
a centre director or supervisor participates in at least 6 hours per year of 
continuing education that is acceptable to a program consultant.

INTENT 
 
The intent of this section is to ensure: 
 the dependable presence of an individual who is able to respond to long-range 
planning and goals, as well as day-to-day operations requirements; and 
 centre directors and supervisors have the required skills, knowledge and 
competencies to provide appropriate environments for children. 
 
ADDITIONAL INFORMATION 
 
The Operations Policy Framework has been developed with additional information 
regarding core services, enhanced optional services and operational guidance to support 
regulated child care facilities in effective and efficient management, financial 
sustainability and compliance within the funding parameters of the $10 a day child care 
program.  
 
Refer to the Early Learning and Child Care Operations Policy Framework for centres or 
homes in the appendices.  
 
POLICY 
 
All Centre Directors and Supervisors shall make application for certification.   
 
Only course work that is post-secondary will be considered for certification. 
 
Only course work completed through an accredited public college or university will be 
considered for certification. 
 
Transcripts from post-secondary institutions within Canada or the United States must be 
sent directly by the institution to the Ministry of Education- Early Childhood Educator 
Certification.  
 
For post-secondary institutions outside of North America, a course-by-course evaluation 
must be completed by World Education Services (WES).  WES must send this evaluation 
directly to Ministry of Education- Early Childhood Educator Certification. 
 
GUIDELINES 
 
Classification 
Classification as an ECE I is granted upon successful completion of a 9-credit unit 
Orientation to early childhood course through Saskatchewan Polytechnic or 9 credit 
units of equivalent education or training.

Classification as an ECE II is granted upon successful completion of a one year early 
childhood certificate from a recognized college in Canada or 39 credit units of 
equivalent education or training. 
 
Classification as an ECE III is granted upon successful completion of a two year early 
childhood diploma from a recognized college in Canada or 81 credit units of equivalent

Continuing Education 
Continuing education opportunities should be from reputable sources like post-
secondary institutions and Ministry of Education sponsored events. Acceptable topics for 
continuing education are those that build core skills to support the quality of the 
facility’s early learning program including: 
 
• Learning that supports compliance with licensing requirements. 
• Implementation of Play and Exploration: Early Learning Program Guide and Essential 
Learning Experiences. 
• Building language and literacy skills with children, including techniques that 
encourage language development for infants and toddlers, use of reasoning among 
preschool children, and active listening and warm communication styles of 
educators. 
• Building relationships with families, including regular progress updates to families 
about their children. 
• Planning and or documenting using developmentally appropriate curriculum. 
• Assessing children using tools like the Ages and Stages Questionnaire to track a 
child’s developmental progress. 
• Responding to the needs of children with additional needs. 
• Building cultural competence, with a focus on Indigenous culture.  
 
Directors and supervisors working in child care centres may also focus their six hours of 
continuing education on content that supports their roles as leaders, administrators and 
human resources managers within their organizations. 
 
Educator self-care or staff appreciation are not acceptable topics for continuing 
education.

SECTION 43 
(1) If a licensee is unable to hire a centre director or supervisor whose qualifications
meet the requirements of section 41 or child care workers whose qualifications

subsection (1) if the Director is satisfied that:
(a) the licensee is unable to hire a centre director or supervisor whose
qualifications meet the requirements of section 41 or child care
workers whose qualifications meet the requirements of section 42;
and
(b) the education plan submitted by the licensee will, within a reasonable
period, remedy the deficiencies in the qualifications of the persons
hired.

FIRST AID AND CPR TRAINING  
 
SECTION 44 
 
(1)  A licensee of a centre must ensure that at least one individual who has 
completed a first aid course is on the premises during the hours of operation of 
the centre. 
 
(2)  A licensee of a centre must ensure that each individual employed in the centre for 
65 hours or more per month as a centre director, supervisor or child care worker: 
 
(a) has completed: 
(i) a first aid course that is satisfactory to the Director; and 
(ii) a course in cardiopulmonary resuscitation that is 
appropriate to the ages of the children attending the 
centre; and 
 
(b) when required to do so by the Director, retakes a course described 
in clause (a) for the purpose of updating his or her qualifications. 
 
 22 May 2015 cC-7.31 Reg 1 s44. 
 
INTENT 
 
The intent of this section is to ensure all caregivers are trained to assess and treat 
injuries which may occur in child care centres. 
 
POLICY 
 
Where there is no expiry date specified on a First Aid Certificate, retraining must occur 
within three years of the date of issue of the certificate. 
 
Where there is no expiry date specified on a Cardio Pulmonary Resuscitation Certificate, 
retraining must occur within two years of the date of issue of the certificate. 
 
GUIDELINES 
 
First Aid and CPR credentials are equivalent to or superior to the credentials required for 
the place of employment as per The Occupational Health and Safety Regulations.

CRIMINAL RECORD CHECK 
 
SECTION 45 
 
(1) Before an individual is hired as an employee in a centre, the licensee must 
obtain from the individual the results of a criminal record check with respect 
to that individual. 
 
(2) A licensee of a centre must: 
 
(a) establish written policies with respect to criminal record checks 
of employees and potential employees; and 
 
(b) make those policies known to the employees and potential employees. 
 
 
INTENT 
 
The intent of this section is to reduce potential risk for children. 
 
 
POLICY 
 
A vulnerable sector check is required as part of the criminal record search. To support 
the need for this check, job descriptions should indicate if a position is one of trust or 
authority over children or vulnerable persons.  
 
Written policies with respect to criminal record searches set out: 
 delegation of authority for review of results of a criminal record search; 
 factors for consideration when determining acceptability of results of a criminal 
record search; 
 frequency of criminal record searches;  
 set out a timeframe for notification if there are changes in criminal record (i.e. 
charges pending); and 
 responsibility for cost of a criminal record search. 
 
Police record information shall be used only for the purpose of assessing the applicant’s 
ability to provide child care services. A criminal records search is acceptable if it was 
done within the past six months.

PART IV – STANDARDS FOR CENTRES  
DIVISION 2 - SUPERVISION 
DUTY TO SUPERVISE 
 
SECTION 49 
 
The licensee of a centre must ensure that children attending the centre are 
adequately supervised by a child care worker at all times. 
 
 22 May 2015 cC-7.31 Reg 1 s49. 
 
INTENT 
 
The intent of this section is to: 
• prevent unsafe situations; and 
• provide continual guidance for children as they explore and learn. 
 
 
POLICY  
 
Children receive developmentally appropriate supervision at all times. 
 
 
GUIDELINES 
 
Supervision is adjusted for different ages and abilities, activities and environments. 
 
Staff are aware of the environment and the capacities, temperament and maturity of 
individual children and adjust supervision accordingly. For example, this may include 
allowing increased freedom and independence for some school-age children, providing 
indirect supervision (e.g. a small group plays a board game in the hallway or an adjacent 
room) as long as the caregiver knows where they are at all times and checks on them 
every 5-10 minutes. 
 
Staff are aware of all children in the group, even when attending to a child one on one 
(e.g. frequently scanning room, frequently counting children, ensuring areas not visible 
are supervised by another staff and ensuring cover-off when needing to leave the 
room). 
 
Staff ensure a balance between children’s need to explore independently and the need 
for adult-child interactions for maximum learning opportunities and safety.

Where evening care is provided, staff are awake and accessible to the children at all

• using a pleasant tone of voice;

GROUPS 
 
SECTION 50 
 
The licensee of a centre must ensure that children attending the facility are 
cared for in groups of children within a single age category unless the licence 
specifies that children attending the facility may be cared for in groups of mixed 
age categories. 
 
  22 May 2015 cC-7.31 Reg 1 s50. 
 
INTENT 
 
The intent of this section is to ensure adequate and appropriate staffing, equipment, 
furnishings and programming to meet the ages and the needs of each child. 
 
 
GUIDELINES 
 
A general assessment of the facility to authorize mixed age categories ensures the 
following: 
 staff-to-child ratios and group size are adhered to (refer to the Centre Supervision 
Charts in the Appendices); 
 appropriate equipment and furnishings are available to accommodate the needs of 
the children in attendance; 
 policies and procedures are established to promote a safe, healthy and quality 
environment; 
 physical environment is varied enough to suit the developmental diversity of the  
children in attendance; 
 daily program planning is appropriate for all age groups; and 
 supervision is adequate to ensure the safety of all children in attendance.

PART IV – STANDARDS FOR CENTRES  
DIVISION 2 - SUPERVISION 
      MAXIMUM GROUP SIZE   
 
SECTION 51 
 
(1) Subject to subsection (4), the licensee of a centre must ensure that the number 
of children cared for in a group while they are indoors in a centre does not 
exceed the maximum group size set out in subsection (2) or (3). 
 
(2) If the children in a group are all within a single age category, the maximum 
group size is: 
 
(a) six in the case of infants; 
 
(b) 10 in the case of toddlers; 
 
(c) 20 in the case of preschool children; and 
 
(d) 30 in the case of school-age children. 
 
(3) If the licence authorizes a centre to provide care to children in groups 
containing more than one age category during the majority of the centre’s 
operating hours, the maximum group size is the maximum number of 
children that can be cared for by two child care workers, applying the staff-
to- child ratio determined in accordance with subsection 52(5). 
 
(4) The maximum group size does not apply: 
 
(a) before 9:00 a.m.; 
 
(b) during the last hour of operation in a day; 
 
(c) during meal times; 
 
(d) during nap times for children who are resting; or 
 
(e) during special activities such as parties or concerts.

SUPERVISION AT CENTRES 
 
SECTION 52 
 
(1) Subject to subsection (2), the licensee of a centre must ensure that there are 
at least two persons present at the centre at all times while children are in 
attendance, of whom one is a child care worker who meets the requirements 
of section 42 and the other is an individual who is at least 16 years of age. 
 
(2) If there are fewer than nine children in attendance at a centre, and not 
more than three of those children are infants or toddlers, there may be only 
one child care worker present at the centre if: 
 
(a) the staff-to-child ratio does not exceed the ratio set out in subsection (5); 
and 
(b) the licensee has made arrangements for the provision of an additional

SMOKING PROHIBITED 
 
SECTION 55 
 
No person shall smoke in a centre. 
 
 22 May 2015 cC-7.31 Reg 1 s55. 
 
INTENT 
 
The intent of this section is to eliminate exposure of children to secondhand smoke 
while in the care of a centre. 
 
POLICY 
 
For the purposes of this section, smoking includes the inhalation or combustion of 
tobacco, cannabis, or any other substance, and vaping includes electronic cigarettes or 
similar devices, with or without nicotine or cannabis. 
 
Smoking and vaping are prohibited by any person (staff, visitors, parents, etc.) in any 
area of a child care centre during the hours of operation. Staff must also prevent 
second- and third-hand smoke exposure by not smoking indoors at any time and 
eliminating residue (e.g., changing clothes, washing hands) before interacting with 
children. Smoking is prohibited in the presence of children on excursions away from the 
premises. 
 
BEST PRACTICE 
 
Smoking and vaping are not permitted in any area of a child care centre after the hours 
the centre normally conducts business. 
 
Refer to The Tobacco and Vapour Products Control Act, The Tobacco and Vapour

additional information.

“The spirit of place depends more on the presence of natural light than perhaps any 
other factor. Like cats, people tend to go where the sunlight is and to prefer rooms with 
windows on at least two sides. Natural light changes slowly and continuously 
throughout the day and across the seasons, enabling us to experience objects and 
spaces under different conditions of illumination. Natural light helps the body maintain 
its circadian rhythms and influences many health related factors and many psychological 
factors such as orientation, security, variety, motion, and change (difference-within-
sameness). 
 
Light affects our total body chemistry. Experiments demonstrate that different colors 
and intensities of light affect blood pressure, pulse and respiration rates, brain activity, 
glandular and metabolic functions, and biorhythms. Phototherapy is used to treat 
neonatal jaundice, psoriasis, and herpes sores. Medical science even has a special word - 
heliotherapy - to describe the salutary effects of sunlight. Insufficient exposure to 
sunlight can result in vitamin D deficiency, demineralization of bones and teeth, 
weakening of the body's immunological defences, and increased stress and fatigue.

PART IV – STANDARDS FOR CENTRES  
DIVISION 3 – PHYSICAL ENVIRONMENTS, FACILITIES AND EQUIPMENT 
OUTDOOR PLAY AREA 
 
SECTION 59 
 
(1)  The licensee of a centre must provide a safe outdoor play area of seven 
square metres per licensed child care space. 
 
(2)  Unless otherwise provided in the licence, at least half of the outdoor play 
area required by subsection (1) must be adjacent to the centre and the 
remainder must be within walking distance of the centre, determined in 
relation to the youngest age category for which the centre is licensed. 
 
 22 May 2015 cC-7.31 Reg 1 s59. 
 
INTENT 
 
The intent of this section is to ensure children have access to an adequate amount of 
safe and secure outdoor play space. 
 
 
POLICY 
 
Where special circumstances exist and a licensee is unable to meet the requirements of 
this section, the licensee may submit a written plan for alternative accommodations for 
outdoor play to the Director for approval. 
 
 
GUIDELINES 
 
The outdoor play area is enclosed with a fence or natural barrier (e.g. dense hedge) to 
ensure proper supervision, injury protection and area control. 
 
Storage structures will be deducted from the outdoor play space measurements before 
calculating the required area per licensed child care space. 
 
All outdoor activity areas are maintained in a clean and safe condition by removing 
debris, dilapidated structures, broken or worn play equipment, building supplies, glass, 
sharp rocks, twigs, toxic plants and other injurious material.

Sandbox Maintenance 
 
Sand play areas should be turned over to a depth of 460 mm (18 inches) annually. 
 
• Use clean sand intended for playground or sandbox use.  
•  Cover sand play areas when not in use to prevent contamination by animals, 
debris, and moisture.  
• Inspect sand regularly and remove litter, foreign objects, and visible 
contamination.  
• If the sand becomes contaminated by animal feces, blood, body fluids, or other 
hazardous materials, remove the contaminated material immediately and 
replace the sand as necessary.  
• Rake or turn the sand periodically to maintain its condition and inspect for 
hazards.  
• Replace sand when it becomes excessively contaminated or cannot be 
adequately maintained.

BEST PRACTICE 
 
Direct access at grade (i.e. no stairs or ramps) from each group room to the outdoor 
play space is provided to create increased use and greater flexibility for children to 
move in and out independently. 
 
The space has a variety of surfaces permitting different types of play, such as sand, black 
top, wood chips, grass, as well as an intriguing mix of trees, flowers, paths, inclines, 
small hills, fences and water to inspire children to engage in creative explorations. 
 
Sunlit areas and shaded areas are provided by means of open space and tree plantings 
or other covered outdoor spaces such as a porch (ideally 3 -5 m deep), veranda, gazebo, 
play shed or hut, lean-to, or roofed activity space. 
 
The outdoor space has access to water (e.g., fountains, water table) for both drinking 
and playing. 
 
9 - 18 sq.m/child is preferable to allow each child enough outdoor play space to play 
safely and exuberantly. 
 
The following areas are included in the design of the play yard: 
 private places that are easily “owned” by a single child; 
 places for small groups of 1-4 children; 
 places for large muscle activity involving groups of up to 10 children; 
 places for games and sports involving more than 10 children; and 
 plenty of free space for children to gather spontaneously. 
 
Play yards are outdoor extensions of the indoor play rooms, providing many of the same 
opportunities as indoor spaces. Opportunities are provided for active, creative, 
structured, quiet, fantasy and therapeutic (i.e. for children with diverse needs) activities. 
 
Outdoor play space has at least two supervisable exits. 
 
Proper drainage is essential for year-round use of outdoor space, with the quickest 
drying surfaces located closest to the building’s entry.
