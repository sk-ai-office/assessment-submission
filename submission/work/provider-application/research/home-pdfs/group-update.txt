Group Family Child Care Home Providers - September 2026 
 
Effective August 31, 2026, amendments to The Child Care Regulations, 2015 increase the maximum number of 
children who may attend a group family child care home from 12 to 16. Section 6(4) of the Child Care Licensee’s 
Manual includes an updated chart with numbers and ages of children. 
This information sheet explains how the group family child care home chart and the family child care home chart 
apply when you and your adult assistant are caring for children in separate groups, and when you are working 
alone. 
What’s New: 
• Section 6(4) of The Child Care Regulations, 2015 permits up to 16 attending children in a group family child 
care home at any one time.  
• Of the children attending, not more than 12 may be infants, toddlers or preschool children. Of those 12, 
not more than six may be infants or toddlers, and of those six, not more than four may be infants. Where 
no preschool children are attending, up to eight attending children may be infants or toddlers. 
• The updated chart in Section 6(4) of the Child Care Licensee’s Manual shows all permitted combinations 
within the 16-child limit. In combination with five or six infants and toddlers, a maximum of four may be 
under 18 months. 
• Attending children include resident children. Refer to Section 2 of the Regulations for definitions of infant, 
toddler, preschool, school-age and resident child.  
• Regulated group family child care homes interested in increasing the number of licensed spaces must apply 
via the Change in Information form and the Expansion Assessment Questionnaire.  
• To be eligible to increase licensed capacity beyond 12 spaces, a group family child care home must have 
operated with a regular license for a minimum of two years. 
• Only homes located in targeted areas most in need of child care will be considered.  
 
Which chart applies to my home? 
• When both you and your assistant are present, your home may care for children up to the licensed 
capacity in the approved combinations set out in the group family child care home chart. 
• Your assistant must be in attendance whenever the numbers and ages of attending children exceed the 
family child care home limits set out in Section 6(2) of The Child Care Regulations, 2015. 
• When you are working alone, the numbers and ages of children attending must not exceed the family child 
care home chart. 
 
My assistant and I care for children in separate groups. Does each group need to follow the family child care 
home chart? 
• No. When you and your assistant are both in attendance at the home, the group family child care home 
chart applies to the home as a whole. 
• Caring for children on different floors of the home or having one group outside while the other group is 
inside, does not mean you are working alone. Both caregivers remain in attendance, and each group must 
be properly supervised at all times. 
• You may arrange groups in the way that best supports supervision, programming and the ages of the 
children. Each caregiver’s group is not required to separately meet the family child care home chart while 
both caregivers are in attendance. 
• Children must be divided as evenly as possible between you and your assistant at all times. Assigning the 
majority of children to one caregiver while the other is responsible for only a small number of children 
does not meet this requirement.

• Both caregivers must be providing direct care and supervision of their group at all times. For example, you 
cannot be upstairs cooking with one child while your assistant is downstairs with the remaining children. 
 
How do I divide the children into groups? 
• Divide the children so that each of you supervises about half of the attending children.  
Examples using combinations from the group family child care home chart: 
o 8 infants/toddlers and 8 school-age children (16): you care for 4 infants/toddlers and 4 school-age 
children inside, while your assistant cares for 4 infants/toddlers and 4 school-age children outside. Each 
caregiver has 8 children. 
o 6 infants/toddlers, 6 preschool children and 4 school-age children (16): you care for 3 infants/toddlers, 
3 preschool children and 2 school-age children on the main floor, while your assistant cares for 3 
infants/toddlers, 3 preschool children and 2 school-age children in the lower level. Each caregiver has 8 
children. 
o 4 infants/toddlers, 5 preschool children and 5 school-age children (14 attending): you care for 4 
infants/toddlers and 2 preschool children (6), while your assistant cares for 3 preschool children and 5 
school-age children (8). 
• Not permitted:  
o Your assistant is upstairs with 15 children while you (the home provider) prepare lunch downstairs with 
the one remaining school-age child. The children are not divided between caregivers. 
o Your assistant is outside with 12 children while you (the home provider) are inside with 4. The majority 
of the children are with one caregiver. 
o You (the home provider) drive 4 school-aged children to school while your assistant stays home with 12 
children. Family child care home numbers must be followed. If the number of children exceed family 
child care home numbers, the provider must ensure there is an additional alternate or assistant that 
meets the requirements in Section 62 or Section 63. 
 
When am I considered to be working alone? 
• You are working alone when your assistant is not in attendance at the home. This includes sick days, 
appointments, vacation, driving children to school, walking to the park alone and any other absence from 
the premises. 
• While you are working alone, the children attending must not exceed the numbers and ages in the family 
child care home chart in Section 6(2). 
 
What if my enrolled children cannot be reduced to a family child care home group when my assistant is away? 
• Some combinations on the group family child care home chart cannot be divided or reduced to meet the 
family child care home chart. Plan for assistant absences in advance.  
• Schedule appointments and other planned absences outside of care hours where possible. 
• Review your enrolment with the family child care home chart so you know which children can attend when 
your assistant is away. 
• Where the number of children exceeds the family child care home limit, the provider must ensure the 
availability of an additional alternate caregiver or assistant as noted above.  
 
Do my own children count in the numbers? 
• If your home is in your principal residence, your children under the age of 10 are included in the numbers 
at all times, even when they are not on the premises. 
• If your home is not in your principal residence, your children under the age of 10 are included when they 
are in attendance.
